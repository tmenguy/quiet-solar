## Primary requirement

**Working on the factory must be cleanly and visibly separated from working
on the product.** Everything else in this epic is in service of that. The
`target` axis is load-bearing; the other two axes organise the work around
it.

## Thesis

**Work is classified on three independent axes, and today's pipeline
recognises none of them.** (Independent, not strictly orthogonal: under
`scale:epic` the `kind` slot moves to the children — see "The axes
compose".)

Today there is exactly **one pipeline shape** —
`setup → plan → implement → review → finish` — and the only branch point is
*which directories does this touch* (`implement-task` vs
`implement-setup-task`), inferred late, at plan time. That single inferred
branch is a degenerate shadow of the first axis below.

### Axis 1 — `target`: what am I changing?

| target | purpose | verification | blast radius |
|---|---|---|---|
| **product** | what ships to the user, plus everything whose job is to guarantee it | tests — a real oracle | users, money, a car that doesn't charge |
| **factory** | what builds or operates the pipeline; ships nothing | split — factory *code* is testable, factory *prompts* have no oracle at all | developer velocity |

**A task has exactly one target. Never both.** See "The two targets" for why
that is structurally forced rather than merely tidy.

### Axis 2 — `kind`: what don't I know when I start?

This axis drives the **phase sequence**.

| kind | the unknown at start | the first productive act |
|---|---|---|
| **bug** | *what is actually happening* | reproduce it |
| **feature** | *what should exist* | bound the design space |

Two values, not three. `epic` is deliberately **not** here — see axis 3.

### Axis 3 — `scale`: one task, or an umbrella over many?

This axis drives the **lifecycle** — whether the issue produces a branch,
a worktree and a PR, or produces *child issues*.

| scale | output | lifecycle |
|---|---|---|
| **task** | code, one PR | branch + worktree + PR; issue closes on merge |
| **epic** | **child issues** and a rationale document | **no branch, no worktree, no PR**; issue stays open until its children are done |

### The axes compose

| example | classification |
|---|---|
| a bug in the quality gate | `task × bug × factory` |
| a new solver constraint | `task × feature × product` |
| a batch of related solver regressions | `epic × product`, children `kind:bug` |
| three regressions **plus** the guard rail that prevents them | `epic × product`, children mixed `kind:bug` + `kind:feature` |
| **this document** | `epic × factory`, children `kind:feature` |

The two classifying axes behave differently under an epic, and this
asymmetry is load-bearing:

| axis | across an epic and its children |
|---|---|
| **`target`** | **uniform and inherited.** An epic is a product epic or a factory epic; every child carries the same target. The separation holds all the way up — that is the whole point of the axis. |
| **`kind`** | **per-child and heterogeneous.** One epic legitimately mixes defects and features — "fix these three regressions *and* add the missing guard rail" is one coherent piece of work. |

Therefore **an epic has no `kind` of its own.** Its sequence is always
*decompose*, so a kind label on the epic would route nothing and would lie
about its children. `kind` is a property of tasks; `target` is a property
of the whole tree.

### Mapping from the issue's original words

Issue #321 asked for three entry points: *bug/defect, features,
dev/process-harness*. They land as: **bug** → `kind:bug`; **feature** →
`kind:feature`; **harness** → `target:factory` — the third "entry point"
was a *target* wearing a kind's clothes, which is why it felt wrong as a
peer of the other two. `scale:epic` was added during planning (directions:
epics have no PR/branch/worktree; an epic can contain both bugs and
features).

## Evidence that the single shape leaks

Four concrete, verified defects (all verified 2026-08-01; symbols quoted
rather than line numbers, which will rot):

1. **Every PR auto-closes its issue.**
   [`scripts/qs/create_pr.py`](../../scripts/qs/create_pr.py) builds
   `fixes_line = f"\nFixes #{issue}\n"` unconditionally. The real victim is
   the epic lifecycle: a **child's PR must not close its parent**, and any
   future fallback PR for an epic document would close the epic itself.
   There is no way to emit `Refs #N`.
2. **The classification signal exists as dead code.**
   [`scripts/qs/fetch_issue.py`](../../scripts/qs/fetch_issue.py) already
   fetches labels and derives `story_type` (bug > enhancement/feature >
   default feature) — and **nothing consumes it**; a repo-wide search finds
   no consumer. Meanwhile [`scripts/qs/context.py`](../../scripts/qs/context.py)
   — the only thing later phases actually call — fetches *only* `title` and
   drops labels entirely. The plumbing was half-built at setup and dies
   there.
3. **Routing is path-based and late.** `create-plan` picks the implement
   variant by inspecting the file paths in its own task breakdown — an
   inference made at plan time, on the wrong axis, invisible to the user.
4. **Epics get auto-closed by the stale bot.**
   [`.github/workflows/stale.yml`](../../.github/workflows/stale.yml):
   30 days → stale, 30 more → closed; exempt labels are only
   `pinned,security,critical`. A months-lived umbrella issue dies in ~60
   days of quiet. (#321 is `pinned` as of 2026-08-01 — the immediate
   mitigation; the structural fix is child A's.)

## The two kinds (axis 2 — sequence)

### `bug`
- **Phase sequence** — `setup → reproduce → diagnose → fix → verify → finish`.
  Reproduction and diagnosis are *phases*, not preamble. The plan is a short
  artefact produced *by* the diagnosis — never before it.
- **Gate — target-conditional**:
  - `bug × product` — a regression test that demonstrably **fails before**
    the fix and passes after. No red test, no fix.
  - `bug × factory-code` (everything under `scripts/qs/`, including
    subpackages) — same rule, in `tests/qs/`.
  - `bug × factory-prompt` (agent `.md` bodies) — **no test oracle exists.**
    The substitute proof of root cause, all three parts mandatory:
    1. a recorded transcript/description of the misbehaving session
       *before* the change;
    2. a **pass/fail criterion written into the bug issue before the fix
       merges** — which scenario, what observation counts as recurrence,
       how many dogfood runs (stochastic behaviour means one clean run
       proves little; the issue states the number). If the scenario cannot
       be re-triggered on demand, the issue must name the fallback proof
       (e.g. the instruction contradiction that was removed);
    3. a **verification tail**: the PR merges but **the issue does not
       close** — it stays open in a "verifying" state until the dogfood
       run(s) are recorded on it. Recurrence ⇒ reopen the fix work;
       observed non-recurrence ⇒ close. This is weaker than a red test and
       the pipeline says so honestly — but it is tracked, not hoped.
  - `bug × factory`, residual surfaces (`.github/` workflows, workflow
    docs, config): the factory-code rule where CI-testable, the substitute
    oracle otherwise — final assignment per surface is part of child B's
    gate profile.
  - A factory fix touching **both** code and prompts applies **both**
    gates.
- **Review lenses** — *root cause or symptom patch?* · *does the regression
  proof actually fail/recur without the fix?* · *blast radius?*
- **Anti-goal** — designing. A bug fix containing a refactor is two changes.

### `feature`
- **Phase sequence** — today's pipeline: `plan → implement → review`. The
  existing flow *is* the feature route; it needs formalising as one route
  among several, not reinventing.
- **Review lenses** — today's plan reviewers (critic, concrete-planner,
  dev-proxy, scope-guardian; delta-auditor from round 2).
- Feature-scope **prompt** changes carry the same dogfood obligation as
  bug-scope ones (stated criterion, verification tail) when they are
  **behavioural** — see the de-minimis rule under "The two targets".

### Why bug ≠ feature (the trade-off table)

|  | bug | feature |
|---|---|---|
| dominant risk | **regression** — breaking what works | **wrong thing built** |
| good change size | as small as possible | as small as *sufficient* |
| the test | proves the root cause (red → green) | expresses the acceptance criteria |
| scope pressure | resist "while I'm in here" | resist gold-plating |
| "done" means | cause removed + regression pinned | criteria met |

(Rationale-only prose — this table informs orchestrator design; it is not
itself agent-body text.)

## The two targets (axis 1)

### The rule: a task is never both

**A single task has exactly one target.** A change that needs both is two
tasks, and must be split. This is a **hard boundary**; the remedy is
`split the issue`, never an override flag.

This is not tidiness. It is forced by self-reference:

> A factory change takes effect **one session later** — you are editing the
> thing that is currently running, so the new behaviour does not exist until
> the next task starts. A task that changes both therefore **cannot verify
> its own factory half**. Mixed tasks are structurally unverifiable.

Second consequence: mixed halves borrow each other's scrutiny. A factory
tweak riding in a product PR is waved through by a coverage gate that says
nothing about it; a product fix riding in a factory PR skips the gate that
actually matters.

**Scope of the hard boundary: `scale:task` only.** An epic never has a
diff, so there is nothing to stop; and epic rationale documents
(`docs/epics/`) are **target-neutral by rule** — they are the *output of
classifying*, not a classified surface. Without this rule a
`target:product` epic could not commit its own rationale doc, a deadlock.

### The boundary: purpose, not path

The classifier is **what is this for?** — paths follow from purpose:

| | **product** | **factory** |
|---|---|---|
| **purpose** | ships to the user, plus everything whose job is to guarantee **or describe** it | builds or operates the pipeline; ships nothing |
| **typical paths** | `custom_components/quiet_solar/`, `tests/` (product tests), `strings.json`/translations, `docs/product/`, **`docs/agents/`** | `scripts/qs/` (all of it, subpackages included), `.claude/` `.cursor/` `.opencode/` agent bodies, `tests/qs/`, `docs/workflow/`, `.github/` |

Purpose resolves the cases a flat path list gets wrong:

- `tests/` is **product** (its job is to guarantee the product);
  `tests/qs/` is **factory**.
- **`docs/agents/` is product** — its job is to *describe the product*,
  and the drift checker ties it to product source (`covers:` frontmatter,
  product-only by QS-185's deliberate design). Classifying it factory
  would hard-stop every product PR that the drift checker obliges to
  co-modify a doc.
- `docs/` root-level files are classified **per file** in the
  machine-readable mapping (e.g. `failure-mode-catalog.md` is product);
  the subdirectory split is the common case, not an exhaustive rule.
- `docs/epics/` is target-neutral (above).

There must be exactly **one machine-readable definition** of this mapping,
which everything else cites. Today the path set is duplicated in at least
five places that disagree (the quality gate's dev-only patterns include
`tests/` *and* blanket-classify every `.md` file dev-only via its
extensions rule; the create-plan routing list omits `tests/`;
phase-protocols words it a third way; the implement-setup-task scope list
and its commit incantation are a fourth and fifth). That duplication is
itself a defect to remove — the full consumer list is in child B's scope.

### The factory splits in two, and this is the crux

- **factory code** — everything under `scripts/qs/` (launchers included).
  Ordinary testable Python; a real oracle exists; `tests/qs/` pins it.
  Nothing special.
- **factory prompts** — agent `.md` bodies. **No oracle whatsoever.** You
  cannot unit-test "will the agent obey this instruction". The only honest
  verification is **dogfooding** — run a real task with the changed agent
  and observe — under the stated-criterion + verification-tail protocol
  defined in the `bug` gate above, which applies to behavioural prompt
  changes of *any* kind.
- **De minimis**: the full before/after protocol applies to **behavioural**
  prompt changes only. Trivial wording edits (typos, formatting,
  restating an unchanged rule) take the parity gate and nothing more —
  otherwise the doctrine is too heavy to survive contact with reality and
  gets ignored wholesale.

**Why this sub-split is legitimately path-selected** (and target is not):
`target` is *identity* — whose stream is this task in, declared as intent
at creation, immutable. The code/prompt distinction is a *gate profile
within* an already-declared target, selected per-diff the way every gate
already selects itself (a Python diff gets mypy; a translation diff gets
the translations check). Inference selecting **strictness** inside a
declared stream is fine; inference selecting the **stream itself** is the
defect this epic removes.

Two further factory-prompt properties: **changes land one session late**
(verification is inherently a *following* task — hence the verification
tail), and **prompt-context cost** — every line added to an agent body is
paid on every invocation, forever; additions must justify their rent.

### Consequences of the hard boundary

- `target` is **declared at task creation** and immutable for the task's
  life. It is never inferred from paths.
- Touching paths outside the declared target is a **hard stop** whose
  message names the remedy and prints the split procedure.
- **`split the issue` is a documented manual procedure, not a tool**: the
  hard-stop message prints the `gh issue create` incantation (title,
  target label, back-link); tooling is built only if real friction is
  observed. The requirement is the boundary; a split script is speculative.
- **Misdeclared ≠ mixed.** A task declared `target:product` that turns out
  to be pure factory work has nothing to split — the remedy is
  **close-and-refile** (or a sanctioned re-label while no commit exists
  yet). Splitting is for genuinely mixed work; refiling is for wrong
  declarations. Both remedies are part of child C's misclassification
  scope.
- Because a task is never both, the two streams may differ in everything
  downstream — gates, agents, review lenses, definition of done — with no
  merge rule needed between them.

## `scale:epic` (axis 3 — lifecycle)

- **Sequence** — `setup → decompose → spawn children → stop`. No implement
  phase; no branch, worktree, or PR.
- **Output** — a rationale document on `main` + child issues.
- **Review** — the plan-reviewer fan-out run against the document
  **in-session, before it lands** (a PR-less review; this epic itself was
  reviewed exactly that way, twice — see "Adversarial review record").
- **Lifecycle** — the issue stays open until children are done. This does
  **not** happen by default (defect 4): an epic must be `pinned` now, and
  `scale:epic` added to the stale bot's `exempt-issue-labels` (child A).
- **Children inherit `target`**, and carry their own `kind`.

## Architecture decisions

### Resolved

- **Three axes.** `target` ∈ {product, factory} — declared, immutable,
  never inferred, exactly one per task *and per epic tree*. `kind` ∈
  {bug, feature} — drives the sequence; per-child under an epic. `scale` ∈
  {task, epic} — drives the lifecycle.
- **All set at setup time**, carried as `target:*` / `kind:*` / `scale:*`
  GitHub labels (six labels total), exposed by `context.py`, routed on
  from the first phase — not inferred at plan time. Setup CLI:
  `--epic` and `--kind` are mutually exclusive (an epic has no kind — hard
  error if both are given); whether `scale:task` is applied explicitly or
  is the labelled default is child A's call, stated in its ADR.
- **Unlabelled legacy issues**: new work always gets labels (setup-task is
  the entry point and refuses to proceed without a declaration); the
  existing backlog is grandfathered — an unlabelled issue picked up via
  `setup-task --issue N` gets its labels declared at that moment.
- **One machine-readable product/factory mapping**, cited by the quality
  gate, the agents, and the docs. The five divergent path lists are
  consolidated onto it (child B).
- **PR linkage.** `Fixes #<own-issue>` **remains the default** for a PR's
  own issue — no behaviour change for ordinary tasks or the legacy
  backlog. What child A adds is a **parent-epic link mode**: when the
  issue belongs to an epic, the PR body *additionally* carries
  `Refs #<epic>`, and `Refs` is the only form ever used toward an epic
  (the direction that cannot close it by accident).
- **Epics have no branch, no worktree, no PR.** The epic document is
  committed to `main` (docs-only) so every child worktree, branched from
  `main`, can read it.

### Open — each owned by a named child

- **Agent topology** *(child A0)*. Honest arithmetic: today 16 agent bodies
  × 3 harness dirs = 48 files under a body-parity rule. Candidate shapes:
  (a) per-kind orchestrators + one shared reviewer pool with per-kind
  rosters; (b) one orchestrator + runtime kind overlay; (c) separate agents
  per matrix cell. (a) is favoured but its file count must be computed, not
  asserted; constraint going in: per-kind delta ≤ 1 orchestrator + ~2
  reviewers, and a hard file budget in A0's acceptance criteria — otherwise
  (a) drifts into (c) by accretion.
- **Routing-table ownership**: **A0 decides it, A implements it.** The
  sketch below is **A0's input hypothesis, falsifiable by A0's own
  arithmetic** — not a decision already made.

### Routing sketch (A0 decides, A implements)

| classification | plan/entry orchestrator | implement | review roster |
|---|---|---|---|
| `task × feature × product` | today's create-plan | implement-task, full gate | today's 4 (+delta) |
| `task × feature × factory` | create-plan, factory profile | factory implement, factory gates (child B's profile; behavioural prompt changes carry the dogfood protocol) | today's 4 + parity/context-cost lens |
| `task × bug × product` | **reproduce/diagnose** orchestrator | fix (TDD from the repro) | root-cause + regression-proof roster |
| `task × bug × factory` | reproduce/diagnose | fix; prompt changes use the substitute oracle + verification tail | same + parity lens |
| `epic × {product, factory}` (no kind slot) | **decompose** orchestrator | — none — | plan-reviewer fan-out, PR-less |

## Decomposition

### How the children run (bootstrap)

Children A0–C are **ordinary tasks executed on today's pipeline**
(`qs-setup-task --issue <N>` → `qs-create-plan` →
`qs-implement-setup-task` → `qs-review-task` → `qs-finish-task`). The
routing they build does not apply to them. Two chicken-and-egg
consequences, stated so nobody trips on them:

- `kind:*` / `target:*` labels on wave-1 children are retro-applied by A
  (aspirational until then).
- `Refs #321` in each child's PR body is added **by hand** (`gh pr edit`)
  until child A's link mode merges — `create_pr.py` cannot emit it before
  then (defect 1). The `Fixes #<child>` line for the child's *own* issue
  is correct and stands.
