# QS-346 — A faulted charger must keep its car attached and visible

- **Issue**: [#346](https://github.com/tmenguy/quiet-solar/issues/346)
- **Branch**: `QS_346`

## Problem

2026-08-09, `wallbox 1 maison` (OCPP), Zoe plugged and user-selected all
afternoon.

At **15:31:56** the wallbox reported a hardware fault and went to
`Faulted`:

```text
15:31:56.147  conn=0  Charging, errorCode: OtherError, info: "Proximity", vendorErrorCode: "HAB:HVB:002:HWR:HBE"
15:31:56.286  conn=1  Faulted, errorCode: InternalError
15:31:56.305  conn=0  Faulted, errorCode: InternalError    <-- last StatusNotification of the log
15:31:56.429  StopTransaction {"reason": "Other", "transactionId": 1786280876}
```

QS detected it immediately — `is_charger_faulted` (`charger.py:4721`) →
`get_charge_type` (`4741`) → `sensor.<car>_qs_car_charge_type` = Faulted
→ red on the car card. **That red circle is what prompted the user to
press the reset button.** But QS also:

- **never notified anyone** that the charger was faulted (the only push,
  at 15:48, blamed the *car*: "ATTENTION NEEDED, ERROR for Zoe");
- kept the charger in the solver and kept commanding it — budgeting
  8→11 A and pushing `SetChargingProfile` until 16:17:28, 45 minutes into
  a dead charger drawing ~26 W.

At **16:51:30** the user pressed `qs_device_clean_and_reset` on the Zoe.
From 16:51:31 the charger logs `no car or not plugged` every 7 s and
never re-attaches any car — not even the generic one. The red indicator
disappeared with the car, because the attached car is the **only**
surface QS has for a charger fault (`get_charge_type` returns
`NOT_PLUGGED` when `self.car is None`, `charger.py:4747`; there is no
charger card, `sensor.py:229` returns `[]`).

### Root cause — a circular dependency

> **To know it is plugged, the charger needs an attached car. To get an
> attached car, the charger must know it is plugged.**

`Faulted` ∈ `_unknown_state_vals`, so from 15:33 the charger's own plug
probe answered `None`. The only compensating path, `_check_plugged_val`
(`charger.py:4395`, rescue at **4428**), asks the car's own plug
sensor — but **only if a car is currently attached**:

```python
if res is None and self.car:          # <-- requires a CURRENTLY attached car
    ...
    res_car = self.car.is_car_plugged(time, for_duration)
```

The reset cascaded `QSCar.user_clean_and_reset` (`car.py:2830`) →
`charger.user_clean_and_reset` (`charger.py:2354`) →
`AbstractLoad.user_clean_and_reset` (`load.py:455`) → `reset()` →
**`detach_car()`** (`charger.py:4280`). With `self.car is None` the
rescue is disabled, both `is_plugged(15s)` and `is_not_plugged(15s)`
return `None`, and `check_load_activity_and_constraints` falls through
its `if`/`elif` (`3568`/`3598`) **in silence**. `get_best_car` has
exactly one call site (`3599`) and `attach_car` one automatic call site
(`3678`) — both inside that unreachable branch. The state is
**absorbing**: only a valid status sample or an HA restart escapes it.

Aggravating: `user_set_selected_car_by_name` (`3404-3411`) calls
`detach_car()` *before* `update_charger_for_user_change()`, so
re-selecting the car in the UI destroys the very `self.car` propping up
`is_plugged`. Design A fixes this implicitly (detach now records the
memory); no separate task.

### Not a defect: the "No allocated car !" notification

The issue also reports a "No allocated car !" push to Thomas. That is
**designed behaviour, not a bug**: `person.py:535` fires it when a person
has a trip prediction but no car has them as `current_forecasted_person`.
At 16:51:40 the user clicked *"Force no person for car"* on the Zoe,
leaving two cars for three people; Thomas was the one left without. No
change required.

## Goal

When a charger is in a state a human must fix (`Faulted`, `Unavailable`,
`Error`, …):

1. Keep the car attached and red, and survive a `qs_device_clean_and_reset`.
2. Take the charger out of the solver and stop commanding it.
3. Alert once per fault episode — the attached car's person and everyone
   in the house — telling them which car on which charger to unplug/replug.
4. Fix the `State`-vs-`str` comparison in `is_charger_unavailable`.

`is_charger_faulted()` (`charger.py:4721`) is already exactly the "a
human has to take care of it" predicate: it tests membership in
`_unknown_state_vals` = `{STATE_UNKNOWN, STATE_UNAVAILABLE}` +
`get_car_status_unknown_vals()` (OCPP `Faulted`/`Unavailable`; Wallbox
`Error`/`Disconnected`/`Updating`). **No new category is introduced.**

## OUT OF SCOPE

- **No reboot work of any kind.** `handle_ocpp_notification`
  (`charger.py:5849`, dead behind a bare `return`) and
  `probe_for_possible_needed_reboot` (`4672`, hardcoded `False`) are
  deliberate user preparation — do not touch, do not revive.
- **No soft poke / `TriggerMessage`.** Decided during planning: OCPP
  status is *pushed*, and a reboot, reconnect, replacement or the fault
  clearing each produce a StatusNotification on their own (that wallbox
  sent 18 across the day). A poke would only cover a charge point that
  recovered but silently failed to announce it — speculative.
- No new entity, sensor or dashboard card. The existing car card already
  renders `Faulted` red; fix A restores that channel.
- The `except ImportError, ModuleNotFoundError:` construct at
  `charger.py:19` is **correct and intentional** — `pyproject.toml` sets
  `target-version = "py314"` / `python_version = "3.14"`, the same
  construct appears at 8 other production sites, and this point was
  raised and rejected in QS-290, QS-243, QS-332 and QS-306. Leave it.

## Design

### A — Break the circular dependency

- Add `self._last_attached_car: QSCar | None = None`.
- `detach_car()` (`charger.py:4280`) records the car it detaches, inside
  its existing `if self.car is not None:` block (`4281`), **before** the
  `self.car = None` at `4285`. "Records the car it detaches" — **not** a
  blind `self._last_attached_car = self.car`, which on a `self.car is
  None` entry would overwrite the memory with `None` and re-open the trap
  (concrete-planner). The point is only that it carries **no** record-time
  migration guard.
- The migration guard lives at **use-time**, not record-time. `detach_car`
  is also the car-*migration* path (called on other chargers at `3202`,
  `3376`, and from `home.py:2037/2048`, `car.py:1466`); at those sites the
  car still points at the *old* charger *when detach is entered*, so a
  record-time `car.charger is self` guard is always true and useless. The
  discriminator only exists **after** the fact: `detach_car` sets
  `self.car.charger = None` (`4283`) and `attach_car` sets `car.charger =
  self` (`4275`), so a **genuinely detached** car (reset/unplug, incl.
  AC 1's Zoe) ends with `charger is None`, while a **re-homed** car ends
  with `charger is B`. `_check_plugged_val` (`4428`) therefore only trusts
  the memory when `self._last_attached_car.charger is None`:
  `car = self.car or (self._last_attached_car if
  self._last_attached_car is not None and
  self._last_attached_car.charger is None else None)`. A car re-homed to
  B is skipped. The rest of the rescue is unchanged, so it stays
  symmetric — a remembered car whose own sensor says *not* plugged
  correctly yields `is_not_plugged = True`.
- **A physical unplug kills the memory** (user decision). Insert
  `self._last_attached_car = None` in the `is_not_plugged` branch of
  `check_load_activity_and_constraints`, **after** the `if self.car:`
  block ends (after line `3588`, immediately before the
  `set_charging_num_phases` call at `3592`) — *not* inside it. The
  existing `clear_user_originated` at `3585` is nested under
  `if self.car:` (`3569`), which is skipped in exactly our scenario; and
  `reset()` at `3584` → `detach_car()` would re-record the memory, so the
  clear must come after both.
  Consequence, accepted by the user: after an unplug on a still-faulted
  charger, a replug does not re-attach — the charger correctly stops
  appearing as an available charger until its status recovers.

### B — A faulted charger is not a solver participant, and idles cleanly

Two levers, plus a verified natural behaviour:

- **Solver (strategic)** — override `is_load_active(time)` in
  `QSChargerGeneric` to return `False` while `is_charger_faulted(time)`.
  The base (`load.py:2108`) is "enabled and has constraints"; it builds
  `active_loads` at `home.py:2943` (the solver input) and gates
  `update_live_constraints` at `2924`.
- **Charger-group amp budgeting (tactical)** —
  `QSChargerGroup.ensure_correct_state` (`charger.py:811`) loops over
  chargers checking only `qs_enable_device` (guard shape
  `if charger.qs_enable_device is False: … continue`). Add
  `or charger.is_charger_faulted(time)` so the charger drops out of
  `actionable_chargers` and therefore out of
  `budgeting_algorithm_minimize_diffs`.
- **"Stop commanding" needs no extra gate — the idle path already
  settles** (round-2 concern, resolved by inspection). With
  `is_load_active=False`, the loop at `home.py:3034` issues `CMD_IDLE`.
  On a faulted charger `check_charge_state` returns `None` when the live
  status ∈ `_unknown_state_vals` (`charger.py:4498`), so in
  `_ensure_correct_state` (`4819`) `currently_charging = is_charge_enabled
  is True` is `False` and, for idle, `want_charge` is `False` → it lands
  in the `# STEADY STATE: not charging and don't want to charge -> do
  nothing` branch. No `stop_charge`, no `set_charging_current`, no
  `SetChargingProfile`, no command launched → no ack-ladder / false
  "lost control". A faulted charger commanded idle *is* idle and the
  state machine rests. AC 5 pins this with a zero-low-level-command
  assertion so a future regression is caught.

The constraint and the car↔charger attachment are **left in place** —
that is what keeps the pair visible and red. `is_load_active=False` only
stops the solver *updating* the constraint; nothing here drops the
constraint or detaches the car (AC 4/5 assert both survive a full faulted
cycle). On recovery the retained constraint simply resumes toward its
charge target — it did not progress during the fault (no energy was
delivered), so resuming against it is correct and self-corrects; no
special stale-constraint handling is needed.

Note the group skip deliberately overlaps the existing
`is_charger_unavailable` guard at `2557` once task 1 makes it live;
`is_charger_faulted` is authoritative because it also covers `Faulted`
and the Wallbox error states.

### C — One alert per fault episode

- Add `self._charger_fault_since: datetime | None = None` (when the fault
  was first seen) and a boolean latch
  `self._charger_fault_notified: bool = False`.
- **Debounce — 2 minutes, one timestamp.** Alert only once
  `is_charger_faulted` has held **continuously** for
  `CHARGER_FAULT_NOTIFY_DEBOUNCE_S = 120` (new module constant in
  `charger.py`, alongside `CHARGER_TIME_BETWEEN_DATA_REQUEST_S` at line
  `209`). Set `_charger_fault_since` on the first faulted cycle, clear it
  the moment the fault clears.

  Rationale: a real fault does not flap, but `_unknown_state_vals` also
  bundles `STATE_UNKNOWN` and `STATE_UNAVAILABLE` (`charger.py:2319`),
  which blip on every integration reload, HA restart or brief connection
  loss — QS-342 documented a car integration reloading twice a day and
  leaving `unavailable` holes of 39 s and 7 min. Without the debounce
  each such blip fires a **critical-alert** broadcast (bypasses
  do-not-disturb) telling the household to replug a car that is charging
  fine. Cost on a genuine fault: two minutes.
- The check runs each cycle in `check_load_activity_and_constraints`, at
  **method-body indentation** (sibling to the `is_not_plugged` test at
  `3568`), placed after the `if self._boot_time is not None:` block
  closes and before that `is_not_plugged` test. (Do **not** anchor
  literally "after 3566" — line `3566` is *inside* the boot block, so
  code there would only run when `_boot_time is not None`.) It sits before
  `reset()` at `3584` so the state machine observes the fault each cycle
  before reset mutates state. The logic is a plain state machine, **not**
  "fire on the rising edge":
  - fault clears (`is_charger_faulted` False) → clear
    `_charger_fault_since` and `_charger_fault_notified`;
  - fault seen and `_charger_fault_since is None` → set it to `time`
    (this is the only thing the first faulted cycle does);
  - fault held and `not _charger_fault_notified` and
    `time - _charger_fault_since >= CHARGER_FAULT_NOTIFY_DEBOUNCE_S` →
    send both notifications and set `_charger_fault_notified = True`.
  Both notifications are **always** issued (no branching on recipient):
  - `await self.on_device_state_change(time, DEVICE_STATUS_CHANGE_ERROR,
    message=<same body as the broadcast>)` — the charger override
    (`2909`) routes to the attached car's `current_forecasted_person`,
    title `ATTENTION NEEDED, ERROR for <car>`. When no person is
    resolvable, `mobile_app` is `None` and it is harmlessly dropped at
    `device.py:749` — it is *called*, not skipped, so there is a single
    code path to cover.
  - `await self.home.async_notify_all_mobile_apps(title, message)`
    (`home.py:1129`, signature `(title: str, message: str) -> None`).
    Broadcast title: literal `"Charger error — action needed"`.
- The **raw status string** comes from
  `self.get_sensor_latest_possible_valid_value(
  self.charger_status_sensor_unfiltered, time=time)` — the same unfiltered
  probe `is_charger_faulted` reads. In the notify path this is **always
  non-`None`**: reaching it requires `is_charger_faulted` to have held for
  120 s, which means the unfiltered probe carries the fault value (e.g.
  `"Faulted"`) — verified by the concrete reviewer. So **no `"unknown"`
  fallback** (it would be unreachable dead code under the 100% gate).
- The message body is pinned so code and test cannot drift:
  - with a car: `"<charger> is in error (<status>) and cannot charge.
    Please go unplug and replug <car> on <charger>."` — car name is
    `self.car or self._last_attached_car`;
  - no car / no memory: `"<charger> is in error (<status>) and cannot
    charge. Please check the charger."`
  AC 5 asserts the substring `"unplug and replug"` (car case) or
  `"Please check the charger"` (no-car case).
- These two fields are plain attributes, **not** part of any reset path
  (`_has_state_to_reset` at `3511` does not list them), so the
  `reset(keep_commands=True)` at `3584` mid-episode does not re-arm the
  alert. AC 5 asserts this.
- In-memory: an HA restart re-alerts once (after the 2 min debounce).
- `mobile_app` is `None` on every device except `QSPerson`
  (`device.py:283`), so the per-person push is silently dropped
  (`device.py:749`) when no person is allocated — the **broadcast is the
  guaranteed channel**, which is why both are sent.
- Notification strings are hardcoded English throughout this codebase and
  never enter `strings.json`: **no translations-gate impact**.

### D — One-line fix: `is_charger_unavailable`

- `is_charger_unavailable` (def `4509`, bug at **`4513`**):
  `state_wallbox == STATE_UNAVAILABLE` compares a `State` object to a
  string and has always been `False`. Fix to `state_wallbox.state ==
  STATE_UNAVAILABLE`. This activates dormant paths at `2557`
  (`get_stable_dynamic_charge_status`) and `4795`
  (`ensure_correct_state`); `3542` gates the stubbed reboot probe, so no
  change there — record the expected behaviour delta at `2557`/`4795` and
  repair any existing test that asserted the old always-`False` value.

**OCPP `Occupied` is deliberately NOT changed** (user decision): the real
meaning of the 2.0.1 `Occupied` aggregate is not established, so
reclassifying it as plugged would be a guess. Left as-is; separate issue
if ever needed.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Re-attach.** OCPP charger with status `Faulted`, the Zoe attached
   then detached (reset button), the Zoe still the only car physically at
   this charger: within one cycle the charger re-attaches a car (the
   scored path picks the Zoe by physical presence, user-selection having
   been cleared by the reset) and `get_charge_type()` returns
   `CAR_CHARGE_TYPE_FAULTED`. The re-attach is what matters; a test may
   assert the Zoe specifically given a single-car scoring context.
2. **Physical unplug wins.** Same setup, but the remembered car's own
   plug sensor reports not-plugged: `is_not_plugged(...)` is `True`, no
   car is attached, and `_last_attached_car` is cleared.
3. **No cross-charger leak (use-time guard).** A car recorded as charger
   A's `_last_attached_car` but now attached to charger B
   (`car.charger is B`) is **not** trusted by A's `_check_plugged_val`
   rescue — A does not report itself plugged on the strength of a car
   plugged into B.
4. **Out of the solver and unbudgeted, car retained.** While faulted:
   `is_load_active(time)` is `False`; a seeded-faulted charger is absent
   from the `actionable_chargers` list returned by
   `QSChargerGroup.ensure_correct_state`; and after a full faulted cycle
   `self.car` and its constraint are **still present** (Goal 1). When the
   status clears, `is_load_active` and budgeting revert.
5. **Idle, silent, alert once after 2 minutes.** Driving
   `execute_command(CMD_IDLE)` on a faulted charger issues **zero**
   low-level commands (`start_charge` / `stop_charge` /
   `set_charging_current` / `set_charging_num_phases` all uncalled — spy
   on those four). Once the fault has held for
   `CHARGER_FAULT_NOTIFY_DEBOUNCE_S`: exactly one
   `on_device_state_change(DEVICE_STATUS_CHANGE_ERROR)` and exactly one
   `async_notify_all_mobile_apps`, whose message contains the charger
   name, the car name, the raw status and the unplug/replug instruction.
   With no car and no remembered car: one broadcast with the charger-only
   message. Further cycles in the same episode: zero, **including across a
   `reset(keep_commands=True)`**. After recovery and a new fault: one more
   of each.
6. **Debounce holds.** A fault that clears **before**
   `CHARGER_FAULT_NOTIFY_DEBOUNCE_S` elapses produces **zero**
   notifications of either kind, and `_charger_fault_since` is reset so a
   later fault must serve the full 2 minutes again.
7. **`is_charger_unavailable`** returns `True` when the status entity's
   `state` is `STATE_UNAVAILABLE` (fails today).
