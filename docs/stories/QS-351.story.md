### Eliminated alternatives (review round 1)

- **ID.buzz was `-3` (covered), not `-2` (data error), at 07:05:04.** Its SOC
  was fresh at 69 % (`update_value_callback … ID.buzz True/69.0 (69.0/99.0)` at
  06:50:47 and again 07:05:48), and at 07:05:04.436 the allocation itself
  produced a real coverage verdict on that car: `Notifying person Thomas
  Menguy … changed_car ID.buzz: OK, it is already ready!`. A `-2` is
  car-level (`is_covered is None` / `diff_energy is None`, `car.py:1993`), so a
  covered verdict for Thomas rules it out for Magali too; Magali's own
  `changed_car ID.buzz: OK, it is already ready!` at 07:08:04.540 corroborates
  it directly.
- **Cache re-apply path ruled out.** 07:02:03, 07:05:04 and 07:08:04 are three
  *full* recomputations on the `HOME_PERSON_CAR_ALLOCATION_CACHE_S = 3 * 60`
  cadence (`home.py:154`, gate at `home.py:2590`); the cache branch
  (`home.py:2741-2753`) only copies the cached dict and cannot create a new
  assignment. There is no "pre-allocation of unplugged cars" path in the code
  (the test module docstring claiming one is stale — see fix plan step 3).
- **Arthur's forecast was not stale.** At 07:05:04 his leave time 07:06:49 was
  still ahead; "99 km on the Twingo" was a genuine need (direction confirmed
  by the gate flip; magnitude fitted ≈ 8.2 kWh, see Repro) that *legitimately*
  made the energy-optimal pass cheaper (Zoe 0 Wh vs Twingo).

### Repro (throwaway, uncommitted) — mechanism confirmed

Running the real `QSHome._finalize_cost_matrix` + `hungarian_algorithm` +
`_compute_assignment_energy` on the incident matrix (persons Arthur/Magali/
Thomas/Brice, cars Zoe(unplugged)/Tesla(plugged)/ID.buzz(plugged)/Twingo;
first-draft inputs — Magali→Tesla 2778 Wh assumed a 60 kWh pack (corrected
below: 74 974 Wh ⇒ 3472.39 Wh, exact) and Arthur→Twingo 7556 Wh was fitted to the logged
diff; every other authorised pair "covered". Kept as the illustration that
first exposed the mechanism; the proof is the inequality below):

```text
[current code] E_max=7556  plugged-covered cost=7556.5  diff(pref-opt)=4778.00 -> energy-optimal
   energy pass : {'Arthur': 'Zoe', 'Magali': 'Tesla M3', 'Thomas': 'ID.buzz', 'Brice': 'Twingo'}
   pref   pass : {'Arthur': 'Twingo', 'Magali': 'ID.buzz', 'Thomas': 'Tesla M3', 'Brice': 'Zoe'}
[penalty = 0.5 (epsilon)]                 diff(pref-opt)=7556.00 -> energy-optimal
   energy pass : {'Arthur': 'Zoe', 'Magali': 'ID.buzz', 'Thomas': 'Tesla M3', 'Brice': 'Twingo'}
```

The reproduced `4778.00` matches the logged `4778.43`, but the reconstruction is
an **illustration, not the proof** — the defect is matrix-independent (review
round 2):

> Whenever Magali→ID.buzz is covered (`-3`, plugged → cost `E_max + 0.5`),
> Magali→Tesla is a real need `m > 0`, and Thomas is authorised on ID.buzz
> (covered, plugged → `E_max + 0.5`), pass 1 prefers the swap
> {Magali→Tesla, Thomas→ID.buzz} at cost `m + 0.1 + E_max + 0.5 + 0.1` over
> {Magali→ID.buzz, Thomas→Tesla} at `2·(E_max + 0.5)` — a saving of
> `E_max − m + 0.3 > 0` for **any** `m`, because `E_max ≥ m` by construction
> (`home.py:2505`). No other cell of the matrix matters.

That makes the "162 consecutive cycles" claim in the root cause a theorem
about the code, not an inference from fitted numbers. The magnitudes:

- **Direct measurement of `m`:** at 07:08:04 Arthur was a `-1` sentinel
  (next need tomorrow → beyond `FAR_FUTURE_FORECAST_THRESHOLD_S`), so the
  preferred pass carried 0 real energy and the energy pass carried exactly
  the swap: `energy diff -3422.69` ⇒ **m(07:08) = 3422.69 Wh**; overnight
  (same structure) **m = 3472.39 Wh**.
- **Closed with the configured capacity** (user-confirmed:
  `car_battery_capacity = 74974 Wh` for the Tesla M3), not the 60 kWh assumed
  in the first draft: `(41.6314620759743 − 37) % × 74 974 Wh = 3472.39 Wh`
  (overnight, target from `charger.py:4041` at 07:05:04) and
  `(41.56516835471106 − 37) % × 74 974 Wh = 3422.69 Wh` (07:07:33 target) —
  **exact to the hundredth of a Wh on both**. No residual remains: the two
  negative log values are Magali→Tesla, nothing else. The needed-SOC drift
  41.63 → 41.565 % between 07:05 and 07:07 is the Tesla's `km_per_percent`
  moving as the charge started (`car.py:1923-1927`).
- The Arthur→Twingo need is then `4778.43 + 3472.39 = 8250.8 Wh` — still
  **fitted** (the Twingo runs on a manual SOC estimate; no SOC line on the
  14th), which is fine: the inequality above does not depend on it.

With a sub-energy tie-break penalty Magali stays on the ID.buzz and no Tesla
constraint would have been created.

## Root cause

### Event 1 — Zoe: **no defect** (user-requested charge)

Three genuine entity actions on the Zoe device at 02:33:07 / 02:33:12 /
02:33:15 (`select.py:332`, `button.py:319` → `QSCar.user_set_next_charge_target`,
`QSCar.user_add_default_charge`) created a `CONSTRAINT_ORIGINATOR_MANUAL`
`MANDATORY_END_TIME` constraint "70 % by 07:00" in
`QSChargerGeneric.check_load_activity_and_constraints`
(`ha_model/charger.py:3948`). Arthur had selected himself as the Zoe's person
at 01:48:45, his forecast was 99 km leaving 07:06, the notifications went to
`mobile_app_iphone_13_arthur`, and the Zoe was unplugged and left at 07:03.
The solver scheduled the 8 kWh in the cheapest window before the deadline
(05:15 → 06:29) and stopped when 70 % was met. Quiet Solar behaved as
designed. **Confirmed by Arthur (2026-09-14, via the user).** Exit for this event: no defect.

### Event 2 — Tesla M3: **defect** in the person↔car allocation cost matrix

**One sentence:** In `QSHome._finalize_cost_matrix`
(`custom_components/quiet_solar/ha_model/home.py:2534`) a *covered* pair on a
*plugged* car is priced `plugged_covered_penalty = E_max + 0.5` — strictly
above every real charging need in the matrix — so the energy-optimal Hungarian
pass in `QSHome.compute_and_set_best_persons_cars_allocations`
(`home.py:2681-2703`) provably (saving `E_max − m + 0.3 > 0`, see Repro) paired
Magali with the 37 % Tesla instead of her plugged, already-covered ID.buzz on
every one of the 162 overnight cycles (negative `energy diff` each time); the
two-pass gate was the only protection, and at 07:05:04 a **legitimate** flip
of that gate exposed it, after which `check_load_activity_and_constraints`
(`charger.py:4041-4163`) turned the pairing into a `MANDATORY_END_TIME`
"41.63 % by 08:30" constraint that the solver executed as an 11 kW grid charge.

Two links, only the second is a defect:

1. **Legitimate:** the Zoe unplug (`charger.py:3619` `clear_all_user_originated()`
   wiping Arthur's manual pairing) put Arthur and the Zoe back in the pool;
   Arthur → Zoe costs 0 Wh (covered) while his preferred Twingo needs
   ~8 kWh, so the energy-optimal pass was genuinely cheaper than the
   preferred one by more than the threshold. Any comparable legitimate flip
   would have exposed the defect the same way.
2. **Defect:** the energy-optimal pass itself was wrong for Magali because of
   the `E_max + 0.5` plugged-covered penalty.

Why the penalty inverts the objective: the docstring (`home.py:2528-2530`)
intends to "save a plugged-in car for someone who actually needs the car
charged". But `E_max + 0.5` is applied unconditionally and is larger than any
real `diff_energy`, so whenever a person's preferred, plugged car already
covers them, the optimiser prefers **any** other authorised car that *needs
charging* — it manufactures charging demand. And the intent was structurally
unreachable anyway: plugged-ness is invisible to the gate's real-energy
comparison (`_compute_assignment_energy`, `home.py:2559-2573`, counts only
positive `diff_energy`), so a pass-1 reshuffle driven by the penalty either
changes nothing at the gate or, as here, smuggles a *worse* real-energy
pairing through when an unrelated saving flips the gate. A tie-break is all
the cost matrix can usefully carry for this preference.

Why it was transient (3 min): Arthur's "99 km leaving 07:06" need plus the
freed Zoe were in the pool only until 07:06:52, when his next need moved to
tomorrow; the 07:08 recomputation returned to the preferred assignment. The
Tesla had already started (mandatory constraint + 1 h 25 deadline ⇒ full
power immediately) and the on/off hysteresis kept it at 6 A until the user
reset at 07:21.

### Contributing defects observed (not decisive for this incident)

B is **fixed in this issue** (user decision: same matrix, small). C is split out to
[#352](https://github.com/tmenguy/quiet-solar/issues/352).

- **B — units mismatch.** `diff_energy` is in **Wh**
  (`car.py:1996`: `abs(needed_soc - current_soc) * car_battery_capacity / 100`,
  capacity configured in Wh — `strings.json` "Battery capacity (Wh)"), yet it
  is compared with `PREFERRED_CAR_ENERGY_THRESHOLD_KWH = 1.0` (`const.py:471`)
  at `home.py:2692` and logged as "kWh". The preferred-car bias is therefore
  effectively 1 Wh: practically any disagreement between the two passes flips
  to energy-optimal. Tonight 4.78 kWh > 1 kWh regardless, so B did not decide
  the outcome, but it will on smaller differences.
- **C — person target leaks into `_next_charge_target`.** `charger.py:4137`
  `await self.car.set_next_charge_target_percent(person_min_target_charge)`
  persists after the person constraint is removed;
  `home.py:2773` only clears `_next_charge_target` when it equals the default.
  Visible at 07:08:04 as the filler `Tesla M3 37.0/41/3/3` replacing
  `37.0/80.0/3/3` until the user reset. (`adapt_max_charge_limit` clamps to
  `car_default_charge`, so the Tesla's native limit was **not** lowered.)

## Fix plan

Minimum-diff, produced by the diagnosis. Two defects, one function family.
The matrix **stays in Wh** (`car_battery_capacity` is Wh) — no unit
conversion anywhere.

**Blast radius — cost ordering before / after** (all values Wh; `n = len(p_s)`):

| cell | before (today) | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| covered, unplugged | 0 | 0 |
| **covered, plugged** | **`E_max + 0.5`** (above every real need) | **`0.5`** (absolute; unrelated to today's `+0.5` offset, which is removed) |
| real need | `diff_energy` (Wh) | unchanged |
| no-forecast / data-error | `E_max + 1.0` | unchanged |
| pass-1 preferred tie-break `PASS1` | 0.1 | **100** |

## Iceberg check

**Not an iceberg — argued, not asserted.** The repro reconstructs the
incident to the logged diff and the epsilon fix is validated end-to-end
through the real pipeline. Structurally, the cost matrix is a small *ordered*
set of offsets — `0` (covered, unplugged) · plugged-covered tie-break ·
`+1.0` sentinels / pass-2 offset · `PASS1` preferred tie-break · threshold —
and the fix leaves that structure intact: it corrects one value that had
escaped the order (`E_max`-relative instead of constant) and the units of the
comparison. Inside the energy pass, the preferred bias is carried by the
100 Wh `PASS1` tie-break alone; that is enough because the pass is only
*adopted* when it saves more than 1000 Wh overall, and within it a 100 Wh
tie-break decides only between equal-energy alternatives. Writing the
invariant into the docstring and pinning it with the `E_max = 0` guard test
is what prevents the next tuning from repeating this.

Known same-signature path, **not** a defect: a car with unknown SOC / no
efficiency data (`car.py:1993` → `-2.0` → `E_max + 1.0`) is also priced above
every real need — legitimately pessimistic (unknown is not "covered"), and it
produces the same negative "energy diff" log signature. Eliminated for
07:05:04 (see Evidence).

Adjacent defect C (person target leaking into `_next_charge_target`) is filed
as [#352](https://github.com/tmenguy/quiet-solar/issues/352)
(`area:car kind:bug target:product scale:task`, back-linked).

Design note, not addressed here: the person-constraint path pushes a
*mandatory* grid charge from a single allocation recomputation, and one
person's legitimate saving forces wholesale adoption of the energy pass for
everyone. A debounce ("assignment stable for N minutes before creating a
person constraint on a plugged car") would have contained tonight's 3-minute
flip independently of A. Worth a `kind:feature` discussion if flips recur
after A ships.
